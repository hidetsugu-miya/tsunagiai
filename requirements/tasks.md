# Tasks requirements

## T-1 Todoist

Work with Todoist tasks in per-project and today lists and a detail view (description, subtasks, the direct parent of a subtask as a chip above its name that has a round checkbox at its left that completes the parent, which takes the open task out of the list too, so its tab closes (T-10), the button of the return arrow and "親タスク" that opens the parent when pressed, and the parent's name, which is plain text and opens nothing, and, when the parent has a description, the description always shown in place right under the chip (the same Markdown view as the task's own description, selectable, cut at the 3rd line with "…", with no button; nothing is shown when the parent has no description; no command and no communication is added: the text is the parent's description of the last load), and a button "編集" at the right end of the row of the tabs (T-10; a pencil and the word, `aria-label` "編集", `aria-haspopup="menu"`, as high as the other buttons of the header) that opens the menu to copy the task link or the description, or delete it; the description can also be copied by selecting its text, which does not start editing, or with an icon shown on hover; a description longer than 10 lines as drawn, a wrapped line counting as one each, is folded at the 10th line, which fades downward, with a text button under it, "… 続きを表示（残り n 行）", that shows the whole text and then reads "折りたたむ", while a press on the text itself still starts editing, and a description of 10 lines or fewer is shown in full with no button), and create a task from a mail. Authentication is a personal API token, and the settings screen shows the connection and unlinks it The row of the tabs has the icon button "Orca に渡す" (A-9), at the left of "編集"; both act on the selected tab. The task's name at the top of the detail is a field that wraps at its width and grows with its text, so a long name is read in full (a `textarea` of one row that `nameField` in `ts/todo-detail.ts` fits to its text; the draft of S-2 has the same field); Enter saves it and adds no line break (a break typed or pasted turns into a space), an empty name goes back to the task's own, and a changed name is saved (`todoist_update`). The detail has two columns, the body (description, subtasks, comments) and the side pane of 220px (project, date, labels); the body column keeps at least 320px, and where the width of the detail (the right side of T-10, 50% of the screen at first and 35% to 65% by its divider, less the 32px of its margins; under the tasks it is as wide as the column left of the project list) is under 556px (320px, the 16px gap and 220px), the side pane goes under the body, with the body first, at the same 220px width and the same look, by width alone.

The row of the task list (`li.task.tall`; a project's list and the flat "today" list) has three lines, so that it reads at half the screen (a list about 460px wide). The first line holds the round checkbox, the mail icon where the task links a mail of a connected account, and the name (cut with an ellipsis where it does not fit). The second line (`.desc`) is the first line of the description that is not empty, in the muted color, cut with an ellipsis, and underlined (`.desc.url`) when it begins with `http://` or `https://` (it is not a link: a press on the row opens the task); a task with an empty description has no second line. The third line (`.meta`) holds, at the left on one line (what does not fit is hidden), the due moment (`.due`, the accent color, red for an overdue task, with the date icon; none for a task with no due date), the labels (`.label`, the label icon and the name), the subtask count (`.subcount`, "done/total") and, in the "today" list, the parent of a subtask (`.parent`); the project (`.ptag`, a dot in its color and the name) is at the right end, only in the "today" list. A task with nothing for the third line has none. The comment count is not drawn (the task has none). The row buttons (`.row-actions`) show over the top right of the row on hover and focus and leave the third line alone; the grip, the mark of a drop, the strike-through of a done task, the edit of the name and the indent of subtasks are as before. Rows are told apart by a thin line.

Failures and warnings of the Todo screen (A-28, #1486). The screen draws no band, line or note of a failure or a warning: each is a card at the bottom right. While Todoist does not answer and the backend gives the list it fetched last (`offline`), a warning card (`setNotice`, key `todo:offline`, the orange bar, no button besides 「ログを見る」) says 「Todoist に接続できません（{reason}）。{M}月{D}日 {HH}:{MM} 時点の内容を表示しています。」; it goes when a load answers from Todoist and when the token form is shown. A token that the load does not accept after 保存 is a card of a failure (`showFailure`, 「トークンを確認できませんでした。もう一度入力してください」), and the form stays. The cards that belong to the screen (this one and the comments' card of T-3) are drawn while the Todo screen is shown, and are taken off, with their ×, when another screen is shown (`setScreen` calls `clearTodoNotices` for every other screen).

## T-2 Nest and unnest tasks

In the task list (a project's list and the flat "today" list), drag the grip at the left end of a row (pointer events) onto another task's row to make the dragged task, with its own subtasks, the last subtask of that task (`todoist_move_under`, which sends `parent_id` to `POST /tasks/{id}/move`). The row under the pointer is marked while it can take the drop; the task itself, a task below it and its current parent cannot. The parent opens so the moved row shows. A subtask is made a top-level task again with the "親タスクから外す" button of its row, or by dropping it on the project it is in in the side list (`todoist_move` with its current project ID, which Todoist makes the last top-level task of that project). The screen changes first and a failure is undone by the reload, as for the other task changes; the depth limit is not checked on the screen, Todoist's error is shown. The subtask rows of a task's detail move the same way (T-6). The command `todoist_place` puts a task in a place among its siblings, for the screens that draw where a drop lands (the screens do not call it yet): `id`, `parentId` (`null` for the top level), `projectId` (needed when `parentId` is `null`; the project of the top level the task goes to) and `beforeId` (the sibling it goes before, `null` for the last place). Rust checks the values against the tasks of the last load and refuses, with a Japanese message and with nothing sent: an ID that is not alphanumeric, a task or parent that the last load does not know, a task under itself or under one of its own subtasks, no project for the top level, a `beforeId` that is the task itself or is not a sibling in the new place, and a neighbour that has no `order_key`. The siblings are the tasks with the same parent (for the top level, the tasks of that project outside sections), without the task itself, in the order of the last load (`child_order`). It sends `POST /tasks/{id}/move` with `parent_id` (or `project_id` for the top level) unless the task is in that place already, and then the Sync API's `item_update` with an `order_key` between the keys of its two new neighbours (none before the first, none after the last), made as Todoist's `order_key` is: a fractional index in base 62, as the `fractional-indexing` library makes it. Todoist's documentation names `order_key` set by `item_update` as the way to reorder a task (a reorder by `child_order`, `item_reorder`, is deprecated there, and the REST update of a task has no `order_key`), so one task changes and the siblings are not rewritten. The two requests are not one: when the second fails the task stays last in its new place and the error is shown, and the reload shows the truth. Todoist's own refusal (a tree that is too deep) is the error as for `todoist_move_under`

## T-3 Comments

In the task detail, after the subtask adder, a section "コメント N" (with a fold chevron) lists the task's Todoist comments (`todoist_comments`, oldest first, deleted ones left out). Each row has an avatar with the author's initials, the name, the date (written as the due dates are), the body as Markdown (sanitized as the description is, and a table in either has ruled cells; left out when it is only the file's name) and, for a file, a card (document icon and file name) that is a link, opened in the default browser, only for an `https://` address (its `title` is the host). The author's name comes from `GET /user`, and from the project's collaborators only when someone else wrote a comment; a name neither gives is "不明". The comments are fetched when the detail of a task is drawn as new (its tab is opened or selected, or the side was away) and again when the Todo screen is shown with a tab still selected, not on the redraws of the 10-second reload, and are kept in memory only (shown while a new fetch is on its way); a failed fetch is a card, 「コメントを読み込めませんでした: {error}」 (`setNotice`, key `todo-comments:{task id}`; drawn while that task's tab is selected, taken off when the tab is left, closed or the fetch is tried again), and no message in the section. At the bottom, a rounded field "コメント" (one line high, growing to 6 lines; Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS) or the send button sends) has a clip icon that picks one file (a hidden `<input type="file">`; another pick replaces it, a chip with ✕ shows it, and a file over 100 MB is refused on the screen). Sending uploads the file first (`todoist_upload`: the raw bytes through Tauri's raw IPC with the name percent-encoded in the `x-file-name` header, sent to `POST /uploads` as multipart with a 300-second limit) and then adds the comment (`todoist_add_comment`, `POST /comments`, with the upload's answer as the `attachment`; blank text with a file sends the file's name, and text over 15,000 characters is refused). The text and the file are kept per task in memory until sent, and a failure shows the error as a card (A-28) and keeps them (a file that was uploaded is not uploaded again when the send is tried again with the same file). Nothing is sent to Todoist until the user presses send

## T-4 Labels

In the task detail's side pane, after the date, a field "ラベル" has a + button (`aria-label` "ラベルを追加") at the right of its header, and under the header the task's labels as chips (a dot in the label's color, the name, and an × button, `aria-label` "ラベル「{name}」を外す", that takes the label off and sends `todoist_update` with the labels that remain, an empty list when it was the last; nothing is drawn under the header while there are none). The + opens a popover (`.menu`, one at a time, in `document.body`) with a field (placeholder "ラベルを検索または作成") over the labels narrowed by the typed text (case-insensitive substring), each with its dot and `aria-current` when it is on the task, in a list that scrolls when long. Pressing a row toggles that label on the task and closes the popover. When the trimmed text is no label's exact name (case-insensitive), a last row "「{text}」を作成" makes it (`todoist_create_label`: Rust trims and checks the name, which must not be empty or longer than 128 characters (a refusal is a Japanese message), `POST /labels` with `{name}`, then updates the task with its whole list of labels, the new name in it) and puts it on the task. Enter does what the row of an exact match does, else what the create row does. The screen changes first and a failure is undone by the reload, as for the other task changes; a redraw of the detail does not close the popover or touch what was typed in it. The labels to pick from (`Todo.labels`) are the personal labels (the Sync API's `labels`, by `item_order`, in the color of their name) and then the names found on open tasks that are not among them (shared labels are not in the sync answer, only in a task's `labels`), by name, in the gray of `charcoal`; a cache written before labels existed still reads. A label's name comes from Todoist and may be a collaborator's, so it is drawn as text, never as markup. Not in the list rows, the add field or the Slack card, and not renamed, deleted or recolored here

## T-5 Count by due moment

The number on the sidebar's Todo button and the Dock badge count an open task only once its due moment has come: its due date and time in local time, 00:00 of the date when it has no time (Rust works it out into `TaskItem.dueAt`, in epoch milliseconds, `null` with no due date, and sets it again when it reads the cache; a time the clock skips is read an hour later; the screen only reads it), so a task due today at 12:00 is not counted at 08:49 and is counted from 12:00 (to the minute). A task with no due date is never counted. The tasks that are counted are the rows of the tab 「Todo」 of the pane of the notices (A-35, #1459). The button's `aria-label` says "期限到来". The "今日" list, its rows and its count in the project pane keep today's meaning (overdue and today). The screen counts again from the tasks last given every 10 seconds with no fetch, also while the window is hidden or a field is typed in. The Dock badge is the sum of mail unread, that count, the unread number of the Slack button (S-8) and the count of the Orca button (A-6), and it has no number when the sum is 0; the screen also sends `set_badge` the due moments (epoch milliseconds) of the open tasks still ahead (`upcoming`); it skips the call only when neither the sum nor the moments changed. Rust keeps the two in managed state and a task started in `setup` checks the wall clock about every 15 seconds (the clock is read each time, so a sleeping machine notices at the next check), adding the moments that have come to the count (`badge_total`), and sets the badge of the window `main` only when the value changed, so the Dock follows the time while the WebView's timers are suspended. When the due date of a task is changed from its detail, the screen puts on the task at once, before `todoist_update` answers, the due, bucket and `dueAt` that Rust gives for the text (`todoist_picked_due`: a day picked in the calendar, `YYYY-MM-DD` with ` HH:MM` for a time, or an empty text for no due date); for other text, which Todoist reads, the task stays as it is until the reload

## T-8 Manage projects

In the side list of the Todo screen, a right press on the row of a project opens a menu (`ts/menu.ts`, at the pointer, one at a time, closed by a pick, a click elsewhere or Escape) with three rows, "移動", "アーカイブ" and "削除" (drawn in the danger color). The rows "インボックス" and "今日" have no menu, because they cannot be moved, archived or deleted. A requirement that adds a row for a project puts it in the same menu.
"アーカイブ" archives the project at once, with no question (`todoist_archive_project`, which sends `POST /projects/{id}/archive`; its tasks stay in Todoist). "削除" asks first, in the popup of `confirmDelete` (`ts/hint.ts`: "削除しますか？" with the project's name, a red "削除" and "キャンセル", which has the keys), and only the red "削除" sends `todoist_delete_project` (`DELETE /projects/{id}`), which deletes the project with its sections and tasks for good; Todoist cannot undo it. Cancelling, a click elsewhere or Escape calls nothing.
The sub-projects of the project go with it, in Todoist and on the screen. The screen changes first: the project, the projects nested under it and their tasks leave the list and the counts at once, and a failure is undone by the reload, as for the other task changes, with Todoist's error shown. When the open list is one of those projects the list goes back to "今日", and an open task of theirs is closed; after a failure the list stays at "今日". Rust refuses an ID that is not alphanumeric, as for a task, and answers `None` when a token is needed.
"移動" opens a second menu at the same place, with the places the project may go to: "一番上の階層" first (not for a project that is at the top already), then every project of the list in its order and indent, except the project itself, the projects nested under it, its parent (it is there already) and the inbox (it has no sub-projects). The row is off while that list is empty. Picking a place calls `todoist_move_project` with `id` and `parentId` (the project picked, or `null` for the top level) and makes the project the last sub-project of that parent, or the last project at the top level, with its sub-projects going along and their indent following. Rust sends one `project_move` command to the Sync API (`POST /sync`, with `commands` as JSON text in the form, named by a new UUID) and reads `sync_status` for it: anything but `"ok"` is a failure, and Todoist's message (and its explanation) is the error shown. Rust refuses an ID that is not alphanumeric and a project that is its own parent, and answers `None` when a token is needed. The screen changes first, as for archive: the project and its sub-projects move in the list at once, a closed parent is opened so that the row shows, and a failure is undone by the reload with Todoist's error shown. The command takes the parent, and not the screen's position, so that dragging a project calls it too.
A project can also be dragged in the side list, onto a place of that menu. The press is on the row's button (a project's row has no grip), by pointer events as the grip of a task (T-2, `pressDrag` in `ts/todo-list.ts`), and a press that moves less than 4px is not a drag: it opens the project as before. A drag that is cancelled (Escape) or released where nothing is marked does nothing, and opens no project. The drag shows the project's name beside the pointer, and the project row marks (`drop`) the project the pointer is over when it may go there, and the heading "プロジェクト" when the project is not at the top level. Released on a marked project, the project becomes its last sub-project; released on the marked heading, it becomes the last project of the top level; both call `todoist_move_project` as the menu does (same arguments, same change of the screen first, same undo by the reload on a failure). No mark is drawn on the project itself, the projects nested under it, its parent, "インボックス" and "今日", and on the heading for a project at the top level, and nothing is called when it is released there. The click that ends a drag opens no project. A task dragged onto a project (T-2, T-6) is as before. The right press opens the menu as before.

## T-9 Search tasks

The header of the Todo screen (`#todo-body > header`) has a search field, always, right of the title (`#todo-search`, `role="search"`: a magnifier, `#todo-search-input` with `aria-label` "タスクを検索" and no placeholder, and, only while the field holds text, the button `#todo-search-clear` with `aria-label` "検索を消す" and an ×). It is there while the list is shown, whichever project or "今日" is open, and not under the token form or the page of a draft (S-2). The screen shows no text that says what to type.

The text is sent to `todoist_search` (`query`) 120 ms after the last change, so a run of typing makes one call with the text it ended on; an emptied field (no word) brings the list back at once, with no call. The answer takes the place of the list: `#todo-list` shows a line `.group` "全プロジェクトで n 件", and under it a row for each task of every project, in the order Rust gives (the order of the last load). A text with no word (empty, or only white space) is no search, and the list that was shown comes back. The × does the same, and keeps the focus in the field. Typing leaves the tabs of T-10 as they are. The title keeps the name of the list that was open, and the add field (`#todo-add`) is hidden while the results are shown. Picking a project, "インボックス" or "今日" in the side list empties the field and shows that list.

What is searched is in Rust (`todoist_search` in `src-tauri/src/todoist.rs`, #1108): the open tasks of the last online load, kept as a copy apart from the lock that a load holds across its requests, so a search never waits for a load (the disconnect drops the copy), by name, description and labels, all the words of the text, capitals not mattering. A completed task is not found. It asks Todoist nothing, so the screen sends no new request to Todoist. A failure of the command is a card (A-28), shown only for the latest call, and the screen is drawn again from the last answer with the tasks as they are now, so the tasks of a new load still show (before any answer, that is "全プロジェクトで 0 件").

A row of the results is the row of the list in the form of "今日" (T-1: `li.task.tall`, the checkbox, the three lines, the project at the right end of the third line, the buttons on hover, the grip and the strike-through), so the checkbox, the edit of the name, the date and the move work as in a list. The stretches that the words hit (`TaskHit`: the task's ID, the project's name, and UTF-16 ranges in the name, the description and each label; the task itself is not sent, the screen has it) are put in `mark` and drawn in the accent color, in bold. The second line is the first line of the description that is not empty, or, when a hit is in the description, the first line that holds a hit, so that the row shows why it was found. A row has no toggle of subtasks (a subtask is a row of its own, with its parent on the third line). A row is drawn from the task as it is on the screen now, with the ranges of the last answer; a task that is no longer on the screen (completed or deleted here) has no row and is not counted, so the row leaves at once, before the search is made again.

A press on a row opens the task in a tab (T-10), and the results stay in the list, with the text still in the field. The screen asks again with the same text after each reload of the tasks (every 10 seconds when something changed, and after a change), so the results follow the tasks; the answer of an earlier call that comes after a later one is dropped. Leaving the Todo screen and coming back, or a redraw of the tasks, keeps the text and the results. The token form (T-1) empties the field.

Tests. The unit tests of `search_todo` (the words, the fields, the ranges) are in `src-tauri/src/todoist.rs`. The E2E spec `todo.e2e.ts` checks the field (its place, no placeholder, the label, the × only with text), the call and its argument at each change, the results (the count, the project of each row, the marks in the name, the description and a label, the line of the description that holds the hit, a task completed here leaving the rows and the count), the one call for a run of typing, the return to the list by emptying the field and by the ×, a task opened from the results in a tab with the results kept, typing while a tab is open, the pick of a project, the hidden add field, and a failure (the last results kept, and the new tasks drawn when the search after a load fails). The unit tests also cover `TaskHit` carrying the ID.

## T-10 Tabs of task details on the Todo screen

The right side of the Todo screen holds the details of tasks in tabs, and the task list stays on the left (the owner's decision of 2026-11-27, #1516; the part #1517; it replaces T-7). The list is never replaced by a detail: there is no back button in a detail, and pressing a row never leaves the list. `#todo-back` is shown on the page of a draft only (S-2, S-18), where it discards the draft as before.

The side (`#todo-side`). It is shown while the list is shown, and hidden while the token form or the page of a draft (S-2) is shown (it comes back after them, with its tabs). It has two parts. The first (`#todo-tools`, over the header of the tasks, a band to drag the window by) is tinted as the header of the tasks is (`--soft` and a 2px line of `--accent`, as high as every header, `--header-h`, A-23, so the two lines meet). It holds the strip of the tabs (`#todo-tabs`, `role="tablist"`, `aria-label` "開いているタスク") and, at its right end, the buttons of the selected tab: "Orca に渡す" (`#todo-orca-open`, A-9) at the left of "編集" (`#todo-more`, T-1); both are shown only while a tab is selected. The second part (`#todo-pane`, scrolls) holds the detail of the selected tab (`#todo-detail`, T-1 and T-3 to T-6) and, under it, the pane of A-9 (`#todo-handoff`), which follows the selected tab. With no tab the pane is empty and shows no text.

A tab. One tab for each open task, made as the tabs of the Orca work pane are (`.orca-tab`, `.orca-tab-btn` with `role="tab"` and `aria-selected`, `.orca-tab-title`, and the button `.orca-tab-close`, `aria-label` "閉じる", beside the tab so that pressing it never selects the tab): the title is the name of the task, cut with an ellipsis, and the selected tab has the look of the selected tab of the Orca work pane. The strip scrolls sideways when the tabs do not fit, and the selected tab is brought into view. The tabs are in the order they were opened, and are held in memory only (nothing is saved, and none is open when the app starts; the token form drops them).

Opening. A press on a row of the list (a project's list, "今日" and the results of T-9), on a subtask row or the parent chip of a detail (T-1, T-6), and `openTodoTask(id)` (the rows of the pane of the notices, A-35) open the task in a tab at the end of the strip and select it. A task that has a tab gets that tab selected: the same task never has two tabs. The row of a task that has a tab shows its name in bold (`.tabbed`, in the list and in the results). Picking a project or "今日" in the project list, or the search, changes the list only: the tabs stay.

Selecting and closing. A press on a tab selects it and draws its detail (the comments are fetched as for a detail drawn as new, T-3). The × closes the tab. Closing the selected tab selects its neighbour: the tab that came after it, else the one before it, else none. A tab also closes, with the same choice of neighbour, when its task is completed or deleted here (the checkbox of the detail or of a row, "削除" of the menu), when its task is gone from the data (a reload that no longer has it, the project that holds it archived or deleted, T-8), and the parent chip's checkbox (T-1) closes the tab of the open subtask. Closing a tab drops what was typed in its description editor. A redraw (a reload, a change) keeps the tabs, the selected one, the scroll and the focus of the field being typed in, as it did for the detail.

The detail as a function. `renderDetail(box, id)` of `ts/todo-detail.ts` draws the detail of the task `id` into the element `box`; the Todo screen draws the selected tab into `#todo-detail`, and only that one. The state that belongs to a task (the description being edited, the folds, the comments and their drafts) is kept by the task's ID, so a detail can be drawn into another place later.

The width. The side is 50% of the screen's width at first and is dragged by the divider `#todo-split` (`role="separator"`, `aria-label` "詳細の幅", `aria-valuemin` 35, `aria-valuemax` 65, `aria-valuenow` 50 at first; the arrow keys Left and Right widen and narrow it) between 35% and 65% of the screen (the CSS holds the same range, with no least width in pixels), held in memory. Under the tasks. When the column of the tasks (`#todo-body`) would be under 460px with the side at its least, the side is under that column instead of on its right, and goes back to the right when the window is widened. The width is not read from the column, which would change with the side's own place: it is worked out from the width of `#todo` (the screen without the side menu) as `the width of #todo × 65% − the project list (220px, none while it is closed or under the token form) − the divider (7px)`, and the side goes under the tasks when that is under 460px; so the place changes with the window's width and the project list only, never with the side's width (no flip between the two). The class `below` of `#todo` marks it. Under the tasks the side takes the width of the column left of the project list, the divider lies down (`aria-orientation` "horizontal", `aria-label` "詳細の高さ", 7px high, the cursor `row-resize`; the arrow keys Up and Down widen and narrow the side), and its height is 35% to 65% of the screen's height (`aria-valuemin` 35, `aria-valuemax` 65, 50 at first), held in memory only and kept apart from the width on the right (`--chat-h`, a plain ratio of `ts/split.ts`, used as a flex-grow). While the side is hidden (the token form, the page of a draft) the tasks' column has the whole height (the class `side-off`). The row of the tabs is drawn in both places.

No new command, no new saved file and no new communication: a detail reads and writes as T-1 to T-6 say, and the screen shows no help text (`frontend.md`).

Tests. The E2E spec `todo.e2e.ts` checks: the side with no tab (the empty pane with no text, no "Orca に渡す" and no "編集", the row of the tabs as high as the header of the tasks); a press on a row opening a tab with the task's name and its detail while the list stays (the other rows still shown and pressable, no back button); a second press on the same row selecting the tab and making no second one; a second task opening a second tab, selecting a tab by a press, the detail following it and the buttons acting on it; the bold name of the rows that have a tab and the plain name after the tab closes; the × closing a tab, the neighbour chosen (after, before, none); a task completed or deleted closing its tab and the neighbour being selected; the tab of a task that a reload drops closing; a subtask row and the parent chip opening or selecting a tab; the pick of a project and the search keeping the tabs; a press on a row of the results opening a tab; the pane of A-9 following the selected tab; the page of a draft hiding the side and bringing it back with its tabs; and the width (the side at 50% at first, the range of the divider 35% to 65% by drag and by the arrow keys, the side under the tasks in a narrow window with the divider lying down and its height range, and back on the right when the window is widened). The specs `orca-handoff.e2e.ts` (the pane of A-9 and its button in the row of the tabs) and `footer.e2e.ts` (a row of the pane of the notices opening a tab) check their parts.
