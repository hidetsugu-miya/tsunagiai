# Mail requirements

## M-1 List

Show the threads in the inbox, split into unread and read, each sorted newest first. The unread rows have a heading above them (`#unread-heading`, in the scrolling area, the pair of the heading "既読" above the read rows): "未読" and the number of unread threads of the list at the left and M-6's "すべて既読にする" at the right; with no unread thread the heading is not shown. The list has a header (`#inbox-header`, fixed above the list in the tint of the thread bar, shown on the list only: the title "受信トレイ" only) and no reload button except the one on the card of a failed load below (the bar `#mail-header` is shown only in a thread). Above the rows, `#inbox-state` (in the scrolling area, hidden unless the list is shown) shows the state of the list in two ways: while the first load has not answered and no row is drawn, a spinner with "読み込んでいます…" (the list is shown at startup for this; no list kept by an earlier run is drawn, and nothing but the spinner and the note is shown until the first load answers, #739); and when a load answered, there is no row and the last load did not fail, "受信トレイにスレッドはありません". When the last load failed (the call, or an account in its answer), `#inbox-state` shows neither of them (the list is not said to be empty), and the failure is one card (A-28, #1485: `setNotice`, the key `inbox:load`) with the text "読み込めませんでした: {the error, or each failed account and its error}。自動でも読み直します" and the button "再読み込み" (`.error-card-action`), the only time the button is there; no line or band of the screen tells it, and an account that failed is told by this card alone. The button loads the list at once, and it cannot be pressed while its own load is on its way; when rows are drawn they stay. The card is rewritten in place when the error changes, and a failure that repeats with the same text writes one line to the log (A-28) and draws no second card. It stays when another screen is shown, the list comes back or a thread is open, and it goes when a load succeeds with no failed account; only the latest load decides it, and a card closed with its × is not drawn again until then. A load that failed (the call, or an account in its answer) is repeated by the sync round, which runs every 10 seconds and when the window is shown again, once the wait has passed since that load (60 seconds, doubled after each failed repeat up to 5 minutes), until a load succeeds with no failed account, which clears the wait; when a sync answers that an account needs a login while the list is not shown (a thread is open), the list is loaded again at the next sync round; a load never switches the screen; a repeated load keeps its card whichever screen is shown; a load that draws the rows again keeps the focus on the same button of the same row

## M-2 Read

Open a thread and show its body. Pressing a row of the list (or "詳細を開く" of a row of the pane of the unread, A-35, or a task's mail link) goes to the detail view at once and shows the subject and the sender that the row has (a thread opened from a task with no row shows neither), and in the body's place the note "読み込んでいます…" with gray lines; when the thread arrives they are replaced by the messages. Until it arrives, the buttons of the row of operations (below: the AI summary, Slack, Todo, trash and archive) cannot be pressed, and "戻る" of the header goes back to where the thread was opened from. A press on another row or on "戻る" while it loads leaves the earlier answer ignored: it never draws over the screen then shown. When the call fails, the error is shown in a card (A-28, `showFailure`, #1485), the note and the gray lines go and the body stays empty (and the buttons stay disabled). When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown. A thread that was opened before in this run is drawn at once from the copy the app holds of Gmail's last answer for it, with no note and with the buttons enabled as for a thread that arrived, and Gmail is asked again at the same time. When the answer draws the same (the subject, the messages and their images), nothing is drawn again, so a body keeps its place and its frames do not load again; when it differs (a new reply, say), the thread is drawn again from the answer. When that call fails, the copy stays readable and the error goes to the log (A-28) only, not in the body's place and not on a card; when the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown as above. Whether the thread is marked read (M-6) follows Gmail's answer, not the copy. The copies are in memory only, never in a file, and are gone when the app quits; they take at most 64 MB (`HELD_THREAD_BYTES`), the oldest dropped first, and a thread larger than that is not held. The copies of an account are dropped when the account is removed, and when the fetch of one of its threads (`thread` or `thread_refresh`) gets the answer that the account must log in again. A thread that is not held is waited for as described above. Why. The owner asked for a faster mail screen: every open, the reopen of the same thread included, waited for Gmail behind the note (issue #614). The header of the open thread (`#mail-header`, as high as every band, A-23) has "戻る" (`#back`) only. The operations of the open thread are a row of icon buttons (`#thread-actions`, the first child of `#thread`, just above the subject) in this order: the AI summary (`#thread-summary`, shown only while Claude can be used), "Slack に投稿" (`#thread-slack`, which opens the post pane of S-28), "Todo にする" (`#thread-todo`), and "ゴミ箱へ移動" (`#trash`) and "アーカイブ" (`#archive`). They are in three groups set apart by a thin vertical line: the summary; "Slack に投稿" and "Todo にする"; and the trash and the archive (a line is not drawn where the group before it, or the group itself, is hidden: no summary while Claude cannot be used, no trash and archive for a thread opened from a task with no row). The row is 48px high with a line under it (the owner's instruction, 2026-11-12, issue #488). Each is a `.icon-btn` of 34px with an icon of 20px, with its `aria-label` as the tooltip, and the buttons sit at the row's right end, the right end of the body (the owner's instruction, 2026-11-13, issue #529). The row stays at the top of the scrolling area (`position: sticky`) while the messages scroll, in the color of the body so that nothing shows through it, and it spans the area of the thread (the post pane of S-28 stands at the right of that area while it is open). The reply field of M-10 is fixed at the bottom of the same area. Why. The owner chose this layout (issue #503, option A): the buttons stayed far from the subject, at the right end of the bar, and the bar is now only "戻る". The owner then asked for the buttons at the right end of their own row (issue #529). The body is drawn as Gmail draws it. An HTML body that names no font draws in `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` at 13px: a rule of the frame's root and body ahead of the mail's own styles, so a font the mail names (its CSS, an inline style, `<font face>`) wins, and so does a size it names on the body or inside it (a size named only on the root does not, because the body's 13px is set under it). A text body (`.plain`) draws in the same sans-serif, not a monospace one, at 13px; with a font chosen for the mail screen (A-20) it takes that family and size, while an HTML body keeps its own fonts (A-20). Line breaks and wrapping of a text body are as they were. The http(s) URL that stands as text in an HTML body, outside a link, is a link too: the text nodes of the frame's document (not those inside `a`, `script`, `style`, `textarea`, `title`, `svg`, `math`, `select`, `button`, `noframes`, `noembed`, `xmp`, `plaintext`) are searched for URLs by the rule of a text body (a URL ends at whitespace, a non-ASCII character or `"` `<` `>`, trailing punctuation is left out, and a trailing `)` only when unpaired), and each is wrapped in an `<a href>` built with DOM calls (the mail's text is never put in as markup). It then goes the way of every link (`routeLinks`: `target="_top"` and the URL as the title, opened in the default browser by Rust), and nothing else of the frame's sandbox and CSP changes. Text that is not an http(s) URL is left as text. A URL that another element cuts (a `<wbr>`, a `<span>`) is a link up to the cut, because each text node is searched on its own. Why. The owner reported that a mail drew in a face unlike Gmail's, and that a URL in the text of an HTML body could not be pressed (issues #494 and #495, parts of #493).

## M-8 AI summary

In the mail detail, an icon summarizes the thread with the Claude Code CLI (`claude -p`, the model and effort of A-2 with sonnet and medium as the default, no tools, no MCP, no settings) and shows a short list of points, in Japanese, on a card under the subject. The card follows the thread on screen: it goes when the thread is left or another one is opened (with its ×), and is drawn again when the thread is opened while its failure is kept; a card closed with its × is not drawn again until then, and it goes when a summary is asked for again

## M-10 Reply field

In the open thread (M-2) the head of the latest message, the first one in the list, which is open, has a reply button (`#mail-reply-open`, an icon button with the arrow of a reply and the label "返信") at the right of its date. No other message has one. Pressing it shows the reply field under the messages, at the bottom of the area, and puts the cursor in its text (once Milkdown is read, see below); the message does not fold. Until it is pressed the field is hidden: it is hidden when the thread has arrived (not under the note of M-2, and not on the list), and it is empty for each thread that is opened: leaving a thread, or opening another, drops what was written and hides the field.
The shown field (`#mail-reply`) takes half the height of `#main`, which holds the thread to the height of the area, and is `position: sticky` at the bottom of it, so the messages scroll above it, and the row of operations stays at the top; at the end of the scroll the last message is seen above the field. The text, with the toolbar over it, fills the height that the rows and the bottom line leave, and the text scrolls inside it; the height cannot be changed by hand. When Gmail's answer draws the thread again (M-2), the field stays as it is, shown, with the text and the recipients kept. The field holds, from the top:

- The kind of reply (`#mail-reply-mode`, a select): "返信" (the default) or "全員に返信". The recipients are chips (`.mail-reply-chip`: the name, else the address, with × to take it off; the address too, as `Name <address>`, for a Reply-To whose address is none of the From's, so that the chip shows where the reply goes) of To, then the words "Cc" and "Bcc" at the right. Pressing one opens its row (`#mail-reply-cc-row`, `#mail-reply-bcc-row`), with its chips and a field to add one (an address typed in it becomes a chip with Enter, with a comma (read from the text of the field, so a comma pasted or inserted by an input method counts too) or by leaving the field; Rust checks the address when the mail is sent). A row with a chip is open.
- The text (`#mail-reply-body`) under them: the rich editor of S-21 (Milkdown, A-26), made by `rich-editor.ts` with the prefix `mail-reply-body` and with no send arrow, one frame with the toolbar of S-21 (`#mail-reply-body-tools`, the same nine buttons and link field), and the field (`#mail-reply-body-input`, the editable part `.ProseMirror` with `aria-label` "返信の本文", with no placeholder and no line of text that explains it), which takes all the height under the toolbar. Milkdown is read the first time the reply button opens the field (not when the thread is drawn); until it is there the toolbar and the field are off, and then the cursor is put in the text. A read that fails shows "エディターを読み込めませんでした: …" on a card (A-28), and the next press of the reply button reads it again. Enter is a line break, and Cmd+Enter (Ctrl+Enter off macOS, unless it only confirms an IME conversion) sends, as "送信" does. While a mail is going out the toolbar and the field wait; if the thread is left and opened again meanwhile, the field of that thread can be written in.
- The files (M-12): the strip of chips (`#mail-reply-attach`) under the text, hidden while there is none.
- "送信" (`#mail-reply-send`), at the left of the bottom line, the clip of M-12 (`#mail-reply-attach-open`) right of it, and "破棄" (`#mail-reply-discard`), at the right. "送信" cannot be pressed while the text is blank (spaces and line breaks alone) or while a mail is going out, and Cmd+Enter sends nothing then either.

Who a reply goes to is worked out by Rust (`reply_choices` in `mail_address.rs`; the thread's `reply`), from the headers of the last message of the thread that is not a draft, the one the reply is threaded to (`send_mail`): "返信" goes to its Reply-To, else its From, and "全員に返信" to that and to the other people in its To, with its Cc in Cc. Each address is listed once (the case of the address is ignored), and the account that reads the thread is never listed, unless it is the only address the message went to. A message that the account sent is answered to the people it was sent to. A Reply-To whose address is none of the From's is the one case where the chip's label is the name and the address (`Name <address>`; with no name the address alone), decided in `reply_choices`. A known limit: whether a message was sent by the account is decided by the account's own address only, so a reply to a mail that the account sent from one of its send-as aliases goes back to that alias (the list of aliases would need a new call to Gmail's API, which is not made). Choosing the kind fills To and Cc again (what was typed in them goes, and Bcc stays). Leaving the thread ("戻る", the trash or the archive) while a reply is going out empties and hides the field at once (as it does when no reply is going out); when the send ends, the thread is not read again and nothing is drawn.

"送信" calls `send_mail` (M-5: the account that received the thread is the sender) with the chips' addresses as To, Cc and Bcc, the thread's subject, the Markdown that the editor exports as `body` (it ends with a line end) with `markdown` true, and the thread's ID as `replyThreadId` (Rust adds "Re: " and the threading headers). With `markdown` true Rust sends a `multipart/alternative` mail (`mail_send.rs`): the first part is `text/plain` holding the Markdown as it is, and the second is `text/html` made from it (`markdown_to_html`: paragraphs, headings, quotes, lists, code, emphasis, strong, strikethrough and links; a raw HTML tag in the text is written out as text, not passed on as a tag, except that the empty paragraph the editor writes as `<br />` alone in its block is a blank paragraph (`<p><br></p>`) in the HTML and is left out of the `text/plain` Markdown, which leaves one more blank line there; only `http`, `https` and `mailto` links keep their anchor; an image is left as its alt text), both as UTF-8 base64. Without `markdown` (a new mail, M-11) the mail stays one `text/plain` part. With files (M-12) the text goes first inside a `multipart/mixed`. When it succeeds, the field is emptied (the text, Cc and Bcc, and the recipients of "返信") and hidden, and the thread is read again (`thread`) and drawn with the sent mail in it. When it fails, the error is shown on a card (A-28) and the field stays shown, with the text and the recipients as they were. When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown at the next load as for any command (M-2) and the text stays. "破棄" empties the field in the same way and hides it, with no call.

An open thread has no right side of its own (the owner's decision of 2026-11-22, issues #1222 and #1223: the pane of Claude Code and the pane to Orca, with their icons in the first row of the side, were removed from it). The area at the left is the whole screen until the # of the row of operations is pressed, which opens the post pane of S-28 in the right side (`#mail-side` and `#mail-split`, with `#mail-slack`); `#mail-tools` and `#mail-claude` stay hidden, and there is no `#mail-handoff` and no `#mail-tool-orca`. The row of operations (`#thread-actions`) has no Orca button, and the reply field (`#mail-reply`) has no way back (`#mail-reply-undo`): nothing writes into it but the owner.
Why. The owner chose the layout with the field fixed at the bottom (issue #486, option B) so that the thread can be read while the reply is written, and the row of operations above the subject (issue #488). The owner then asked that a reply can be written only after an action, with a reply button as in Gmail, and that the field be about half of the screen high (2026-11-15; issue #685). The owner then asked that the reply be written in the rich editor of the Slack fields (2026-11-15; issue #688), and that it go out as Markdown with its HTML (#686). The owner then decided that the pane at the right is not a tab but is switched by the icons of its first row, as the Todo screen's is, between Claude Code (A-36) and the pane to Orca (2026-11-12; issue #490), and that Claude Code reads and writes the thread, the recipients, the subject and the text of the reply or the new mail, the owner can take a write back, and nothing is sent by it. The owner then removed both panes from the thread, because the pane of Claude is not needed there and the pane to Orca is hardly used (2026-11-22; issues #1222 and #1223); the new mail keeps the pane of Claude Code (M-11). Attaching files is M-12.

Tests (#1223). E2E `thread-claude.e2e.ts` checks that an open thread has no right side (while the pane of S-28 is closed), no Claude pane and no Orca icon or pane, and `thread.e2e.ts` the row of operations without the Orca button.

## M-11 New mail

The header of the inbox (`#inbox-header`) has "作成" (`#mail-compose`, an icon button with a pencil). Pressing it opens the screen that writes a new mail in the area of the detail view (M-2): `#new-mail` takes the place of the thread in `#main`, and it is not a dialog. The header of the list gives way to a header of its own (`#new-mail-header`, a band like `#mail-header`, A-23) with "戻る" (`#new-mail-back`), the title "新規メッセージ" and "破棄" (`#new-mail-discard`) at the right. The screen is empty each time it is opened. It holds, from the top:

- "差出人": the account the mail goes from (M-5): the Google accounts, then the iCloud accounts (M-13; the screen asks `icloud_accounts` each time it opens, so a mail can be written with no Google account). With one account it is only shown (`#new-mail-from-text`); with several it is a select (`#new-mail-from-select`) of them, the first being chosen when the screen opens.
- "宛先", with the words "Cc" and "Bcc" at the right, and the rows of Cc and Bcc that they open (`#new-mail-cc-row`, `#new-mail-bcc-row`): the recipients as chips, made and opened as those of the reply field (M-10) are, by the same code (`ts/mail-recipients.ts`): an address typed in the field becomes a chip with Enter, a comma or by leaving the field, × takes a chip off, and Rust checks the addresses when the mail is sent. The chips start empty.
- "件名" (`#new-mail-subject`), and the text (`#new-mail-body`, plain text) under the rows, which takes the height that is left.
- The files (M-12): the strip of chips (`#new-mail-attach`) under the text, hidden while there is none.
- "送信" (`#new-mail-send`) in a band fixed at the bottom of the area (as `#mail-reply` is, M-10), with the clip of M-12 (`#new-mail-attach-open`) right of it. It cannot be pressed while the text is blank or while a mail is going out.

The screen shows no placeholder and no line that explains a field.

"送信" calls `send_mail` (M-10) with the chosen account, the chips' addresses as To, Cc and Bcc, the subject and the text, and no `replyThreadId` and no `markdown` (the mail is plain text). When it succeeds, the notice "送信しました" is shown and the inbox is shown again. When it fails, the error is shown on a card (A-28) and what was written stays. When the answer says the account must log in again, the list is shown at the next load as for any command (M-2) and what was written stays. A mail that goes out after the screen was left (by the sidebar, say) shows the notice and changes nothing else.

"戻る" and "破棄" show the inbox again and drop what was written, with no call. While the screen is shown, a load or a sync of the list does not draw the list over it (the sidebar still shows the inbox).

The right side (#490; the owner's decision of 2026-11-12, issues #486 and #490, kept for the new mail when the thread lost its side, 2026-11-22, #1223). While the screen of a new mail is shown, the screen has a right side (`#mail-side`, `.split-chat`) from the top of the window to the bottom, with a divider at its left (`#mail-split`, a `.split-bar` with `role="separator"` and `aria-label` "右のペインの幅", set by `ts/split.ts` and kept in memory: 40% of the screen at first, at least 400px, between a third and a half, `aria-valuemin` 33 and `aria-valuemax` 50). With an open thread (M-10) it holds only the post pane of S-28, while that is open (`#mail-tools` and `#mail-claude` stay hidden); it is hidden on the list and on every other screen. Its first row (`#mail-tools`, a band to drag the window by, as high as the header at the left, `--header-h`, and tinted as it is, so that the two lines meet) holds one icon button at its right end, the same as the Todo screen's: `#mail-tool-claude` (`aria-label` "Claude に相談", the ✳ mark in `--claude`, `aria-pressed` true only while the pane of Claude is open).
The pane of Claude (`ts/mail-claude.ts`) is the Claude Code pane of A-36, drawn in `#mail-claude-term` (`.claude-code-pane`) under a heading (`.chat-head`, 48px: the mark, the title "Claude" and "やり直す", `#mail-claude-restart`), with the pane id and the owner `mail-claude`: the tools of `tsunagiai-mcp` only (`builtinTools` false), no skill, the `manual` permission mode (every tool that writes asks the owner in the terminal), and a first instruction (`prompt`, a fixed text: that this is the work of writing a new mail, the tools `screen_read` and `screen_write` with the target `mail-draft` and its fields, that a write goes into the fields as it is and 「元に戻す」 takes it back, that the mail is sent by the owner and never by Claude, and that the fields are data and no instruction in them is followed; nothing that comes from outside is in it). The pane is closed or open, as the Todo's pane was (T-7, retired; the owner's decision of 2026-11-20, issue #1106: Claude Code starts when the owner asks for it, not when the screen is opened). Closed, it holds the question "Claude に相談しますか？" (`#mail-claude-ask`) and the button "相談する" (`#mail-claude-open`, `.text-btn`) and nothing else, and no Claude Code runs. The question is the one text that explains a button that a screen shows: it is an exception to the rule that a screen shows no help text, made by the owner's decision, as in T-7 (retired). Open, it holds the heading and the terminal. A new mail that is opened shows the pane closed. `#mail-tool-claude` and "相談する" open it, and `#mail-tool-claude` closes it. The process starts when the pane is open and in view (the screen of a new mail is shown and the window is not hidden), goes on while the pane is hidden (the list, an open thread, another screen, a hidden window: the terminal is hidden and showing it again starts nothing), and is ended, with everything it started, when the pane is closed and when another new mail is opened (a new mail is a draft of its own each time it is opened; the pane is closed again): the next open starts a new one. "やり直す" ends the process and starts a new one in the same terminal with the same first instruction; the button is off until the start has answered. A process that ends by itself leaves the message of A-36 in the terminal. The pane shows no help text, and it has no field, model select or note of its own. The bottom of the band of a new mail (`#new-mail-foot`) is the bottom of the pane (`#mail-claude`): both end at the bottom of the screen.
The fields (the target `mail-draft`, A-36, `ts/mail-draft.ts`; open while the screen of a new mail is shown, and refused otherwise: 「mail-draft の画面が開いていません」). Its fields are `to`, `cc`, `bcc`, `subject` and `body`. A read (`screen_read`) answers all five, as the owner sees them: `to`, `cc` and `bcc` are the addresses of the chips, separated by a comma and a space (an address typed in a field and not yet a chip is made a chip first), `subject` is the field's text and `body` is the text of `#new-mail-body`. A write (`screen_write`) puts the texts of the fields it names into the fields as they are, and the fields it leaves out stay: `to`, `cc` and `bcc` replace the chips of that row with the addresses of the text (separated by commas, semicolons or line breaks that are not inside double quotes or angle brackets; blank ones are left out; an address that a chip showed keeps that chip's label), and open the Cc or Bcc row for them; `subject` and `body` replace the text. It sends nothing and saves nothing: only "送信" sends. Refused, in Japanese, with nothing written: a field that the target does not have (「欄 … はありません」, the field `thread` of the earlier reply side too), a write while a mail is going out (「送信の最中のため、書けません」) and a write when the screen of a new mail is not shown. A write that changes nothing makes no way back. The sender is the account the screen chose, and is not a field. Claude Code never sends: "送信" is the owner's.
「元に戻す」 (`#new-mail-undo` in the band at the bottom, right of "送信", `.text-btn`, shown only while there is a way back). Each write that changes a field keeps what the fields it named held before it, and the texts of all fields after it. 「元に戻す」 puts the texts of the newest write back (the fields that write named only) and forgets it, so the writes are taken back one by one, newest first. It is shown only while the fields hold what the newest write put there: a change of the owner's (typing, a chip taken off) makes every way back unusable at once, and they are forgotten (the screen draws it again at each such change). A send, "戻る", "破棄", leaving the screen or opening another new mail forgets them too. The press is off while a mail goes out. A new mail is a draft of its own each time the screen is opened, so the process of Claude Code of an earlier new mail ends, and the pane is closed until the owner opens it.

## M-12 Attach files

A reply (M-10) and a new mail (M-11) can carry files. A file is attached in three ways, and all three end in the same chip:

- The clip (`#mail-reply-attach-open` in the foot of the reply, `#new-mail-attach-open` in the band of a new mail, `.icon-btn`, `aria-label` "ファイルを添付", right of "送信"). Pressing it calls `mail_attach_pick`: Rust opens the dialog that picks files (`tauri-plugin-dialog`, one or several), reads the files the owner picked, and answers each as its name, its size and its bytes as base64; an empty list when the dialog was cancelled. The place of the files is the dialog's alone: the page gives no path, so the page cannot make Rust read a file of its own choosing.
- A drop on the field (`#mail-reply`, `#new-mail`), which the page takes as `File` objects (the window's own file drop is off, `ts/orca-attach.ts`), and a paste in the field or its text (a paste with no file is left alone, so the text pastes as before; a file copied in Finder is a file). The page asks Rust first, with `mail_attach_check(name, size, held)`, and reads the file only when Rust accepts it; a file with no name is called "file".
- The files of one take are added one after another, each checked against the ones before it.

The chips (`#mail-reply-attach`, between the text and the foot; `#new-mail-attach`, between the text and the band; hidden while there is no file) show for each file, in the order they were attached, its name, its size ("980 B", "12 KB", "1.5 MB") and a × (`aria-label` "{name} を外す") that takes it off. A file that Rust refuses is not attached, and the refusal is shown on a card (A-28) as it is (a Japanese message); the rest of the take stops. The screen shows no help text (`frontend.md`).

What Rust refuses (`check_attachment` in `mail_send.rs`, at the check of a file and again when the mail is sent): a name that is blank or longer than 255 bytes, a file with no byte, and a total of the files of the mail over the limit of the account that sends it (`mail::attachment_limit`: 25 MB for a Gmail account, 25 × 1024 × 1024 bytes, Gmail's limit: 「添付の合計は 25 MB までです」; for an iCloud account what the size that iCloud's SMTP takes leaves after base64, about 19.7 MB, shown as 「添付の合計は 19 MB までです」, M-13). The screen gives the account to `mail_attach_check` and `mail_attach_pick` (the account of the thread for a reply, the sender chosen for a new mail), and `send_mail` checks the files again with the account it is given. A file taken off frees its share.

The files are held in memory only: the page keeps each as base64 until the mail goes out or the field is let go, and Rust keeps none (`mail_attach_pick` and `mail_attach_check` store nothing). No file, no name and no byte of them is written to a file or to the log. The files are let go when the reply is sent, discarded ("破棄"), or its thread is left or another is opened, and when a new mail is sent, "戻る" or "破棄" is pressed, or the screen is opened again. A mail that fails to send, or whose account must log in again, keeps them. While a mail goes out, the clip and the × are off and a drop or a paste adds nothing; a read that ends after its mail was let go adds nothing.

"送信" calls `send_mail` with `attachments`, a list of `{name, data}` (the name as the file was named, the data as base64) in the order of the chips, left out when there is no file (so a mail with no file is the call of M-10 and M-11 as it was). "送信" is still off while the text is blank: files alone do not send a mail. Rust (`mail_send.rs`) decodes and checks every file (a base64 that does not decode refuses the mail, and so does a total over 25 MB, before anything is asked of Gmail) and builds a `multipart/mixed` message: the first part is the text exactly as M-10 and M-11 build it (`text/plain`, or `multipart/alternative` when `markdown` is true), and each file follows as a part of `application/octet-stream` in base64 lines of 76 characters, with `Content-Disposition: attachment`. The name goes into the header only encoded: `filename="…"` holds the name with every character that is not printable ASCII (and every `"` and `\`) turned into `_`, and, when that changed the name, `filename*=UTF-8''…` (RFC 2231) holds the whole name percent-encoded, each parameter on a line of its own, so that no line break in a name can start a header or end the part. The boundaries are fixed texts (`tsunagiai-mixed` for the message, `tsunagiai-alternative` inside), which no base64 line can hold.

Why. The owner decided that files can be attached to a reply and to a new mail (2026-11-12; issue #486, and #491 for this part): from the clip beside "送信", or by a drop or a paste in the field, shown as chips under the text, with a ×.

Tests (#491). `cargo test`: in `mail_send.rs`, the message with files (the `multipart/mixed` headers, the text part first and the bytes of each file back, the lines of 76, the `multipart/alternative` kept inside, no change for a mail with no file or an empty list), the names (non-ASCII, a quote and a backslash, a line break that starts no header, a name of 255 bytes), the limit (exactly 25 MB passes, one byte over does not, the files already held count, a base64 over the limit is refused before it is decoded, a base64 that does not decode, an empty file, a blank or too long name), and `attachments` left out of the JSON; in `mail_attach.rs`, the files read from temporary files with their names, sizes and bytes, one that may not be added or cannot be read (an empty file, a folder, a missing file, a total over the limit, a file over the limit that is refused before it is read, a path with no name) refusing all of them. The dialog itself is not run. E2E `thread-attach.e2e.ts`: for the reply and for a new mail, the clip and the strip in their places, a pick (the chips with their names and sizes, the bytes already held given to the next pick, a cancelled dialog), a drop and a paste (the check of Rust with the name, the size and the bytes held before, a paste of text left alone, the drop outline gone), a refusal on a card, the × taking one file off, the files in `send_mail` in order and let go after it, kept when the send fails, the clip, the × and a drop off while the mail goes out, and let go by "破棄", "戻る", leaving the thread and a pick that ends after its field was let go.

## M-13 iCloud accounts

An iCloud mail address can be added as an account beside the Gmail accounts, with the address and an App-specific password (the owner's request, 2026-11-28; #1571). This is the part of M-13 that adds, checks and removes the account (#1573), the part that shows its inbox and its threads (#1574), the part that syncs it (#1575) the part that marks read, deletes and archives and takes the last two back (#1576) and the part that sends, replies and attaches files (#1577) and the part of the MCP tools and the Todo link (#1578). The threads of an iCloud account are in the list of mail with the Gmail ones; the account is in no calendar, and the Google card shows only Gmail accounts.

iCloud has no mail API and no OAuth for other apps, so the account is IMAP to `imap.mail.me.com:993` over TLS, logged in with the full address and the App-specific password (made at account.apple.com; it needs the two-factor authentication of the Apple Account, and changing the Apple Account's password makes every App-specific password invalid).

The settings have an iCloud tab (A-3) with one card: the header row (the logo, 「iCloud」, and the `?` hint, `#icloud-help`, with the steps of making an App-specific password and the note about the password), a row for each account, and the add form (`#icloud-add`: the address, `#icloud-address`, the App-specific password, `#icloud-password`, and 「追加」, `#icloud-add-button`; the fields only name themselves with their placeholders). A row has the avatar, the address, a line, a state label and the buttons:

- The tab reads the accounts each time it is shown (`icloud_accounts`), draws them with the state 「確認中」 (gray), and asks iCloud about each account's kept password (`icloud_check`: an IMAP login with the password of the keychain, then a logout; nothing is written). The answer sets the label: 「接続中」 (green) when iCloud accepts the login, 「入れ直しが必要」 (orange, with the line 「App 用パスワードが無効になりました」 and the button 「入れ直す」) when iCloud refuses it or no password is kept, and 「確認できません」 (orange, with the reason as the line) when iCloud could not be asked (no network, a broken TLS, a timeout of 20 seconds). Only the latest read may draw: an answer that arrives after another read, an add or an unlink began is dropped.
- 「追加」 sends the address and the password (`icloud_add_account`). The password field is emptied as soon as the text is read, whatever the answer is, and the button cannot be pressed while the call is on its way. Rust trims the address and writes it in lower case, refuses one that is not `name@domain` and refuses an empty password or one with a control character, then logs in to iCloud first, and only when iCloud accepts it keeps the password in the OS keychain (service `jp.mysis.tsunagiai.icloud`, user the address) and adds the row to `accounts.json` with the kind `icloud` (an address that is listed already stays one row). The rows then show 「接続中」 with no second login. When iCloud refuses the login the error is 「メールアドレスか App 用パスワードが違います」; when it cannot be reached, the error says so in Japanese. An error is shown on a card (A-28), the list and the address field stay as they were, and nothing is kept. An address of a Gmail account is refused (「{address} は Google のアカウントとして登録されています」).
- 「入れ直す」 puts the row's address in the address field and moves the focus to the password field; the add that follows enters the password again (the same `icloud_add_account`), which replaces the keychain item and keeps the row.
- 「解除」 asks first (the confirmation of the other cards: 「{address} の接続を解除しますか？」, 「App 用パスワードをつなぎAIから削除します。iCloud のメールは残ります。」, 「解除」 and 「キャンセル」), then `icloud_remove_account` deletes the password in the keychain and the row, and the card draws the accounts that are left. A failure is shown on a card and the row stays. A row that is not an iCloud account is refused.

With no account the card says 「アカウントはまだありません」. The password is never shown, logged, written to a file, returned by a command or put in an error text: the screen keeps no copy of it after it has been sent, and Rust passes it only to the IMAP LOGIN and the keychain.

Why. iCloud mail has neither an API nor an OAuth for other apps, so the App-specific password is the only way in; it is tried at once so that a wrong one is never kept, and the state is asked on each showing of the tab because Apple makes every App-specific password invalid when the Apple Account's password changes.

The inbox and the threads (#1574).

- The list: the newest 50 mails of INBOX (the number of threads that Gmail's list reads), with the mails of Sent Messages that belong to the same threads added, one row for each thread. The row (sender, subject, snippet, time, unread) is the same as a Gmail thread's. With no Google account and only iCloud accounts, the list is shown, and the login screen is shown only when there is no account of either kind (decided from the Google accounts of `inbox` and `icloud_accounts`).
- The thread: the mails are joined in the app by Message-ID, In-Reply-To and References; a mail with none of these headers is a thread of its own. The ID of a thread is made from the Message-ID of its root mail (hexadecimal).
- Opening does not read: the mails are read with EXAMINE (read only) and BODY.PEEK, so opening a thread changes no flag at iCloud.
- The body is the HTML when the mail has it, else the text. 「テキストで読む」 takes the text first. The images inside the mail (`cid:`) are embedded when the thread is read. An image from outside is asked about each time (「表示するか」): iCloud gives no mark that vouches for the sender, so nothing is shown without the press (Gmail's mark of mx.google.com has no counterpart).
- The AI summary (M-8), the choice of the reply's destination (M-10) and the images take the same shape as a Gmail thread's, and are not changed.
- When an iCloud account could not be read (the password is invalid, or the network failed), the card of the list says so. When the password is invalid, the words are 「App 用パスワードが無効です。設定の iCloud タブで入れ直してください」. The row of Google's login again is not shown.
- The sync (#1575): an iCloud account is synced about every 10 seconds with the Gmail accounts (M-7), through the same `sync` command and the same screen code: the list adds, replaces and takes out rows as it does for Gmail, and the position that the load gives (`historyIds`) is a string that the screen keeps and gives back (for iCloud, the UIDVALIDITY and the HIGHESTMODSEQ of INBOX). Only a change of INBOX is seen: a new mail, a change of flags, and a mail that left. iCloud is asked for the difference since the position and nothing more when nothing changed, and the inbox is read again when that is cheaper or needed (the UIDVALIDITY changed, a mail left, many mails changed). A refused password reads the list again, which tells the account on the card; a server that cannot be reached makes the account wait, longer each time (20 seconds up to 5 minutes), with nothing on the screen. The details are in `requirements/constraints.md`, Mail.
- Read, unread, archive, delete and their undo (#1576): the screen calls the same commands as for Gmail (`set_read`, `archive`, `unarchive`, `trash`, `untrash`), with the iCloud address and the thread's ID, so the buttons, the notices, 「元に戻す」 and Cmd+Z, the mark-all-read and the read mark on opening a thread work as they do for Gmail (M-3, M-4, M-6). Only the mails that the thread has in INBOX change; the replies in Sent Messages stay where they are.
  - Read and unread: `\Seen` is set or taken off the INBOX mails of the thread. A thread that has no mail in INBOX has nothing to mark and is no error.
  - Archive: the INBOX mails of the thread go to `Archive`; delete: to `Deleted Messages`. iCloud has no MOVE, so each mail is copied (`UID COPY`), and only after iCloud accepted the copy the original is marked `\Deleted` and removed with `UID EXPUNGE` of those UIDs. A refused copy changes nothing.
  - The undo (`unarchive`, `untrash`) finds the thread again by its Message-ID in `Archive` or `Deleted Messages`, and moves the mails that the thread has there back to INBOX; the mails of the thread that are in INBOX are not touched. A mail with no Message-ID is a thread by its place, which a move changes, so such a thread can be archived or deleted but not taken back (「スレッドが見つかりません」).
  - A thread that is not in the folder it is to leave is an error (「スレッドが見つかりません」). A password that iCloud refuses answers like the Gmail operations do for a login that is needed (the screen reads the list again, and the card tells the account).
  - The sync (#1575) sees the mails leave INBOX and the flags change, so a row that another device archived or read is updated.
- Sending, replying and attaching files (#1577): "送信" of a reply (M-10) and of a new mail (M-11) calls the same `send_mail` with the iCloud address as the account, and Rust sends through iCloud's SMTP (`icloud::send`, `icloud/send.rs`) instead of Gmail's API. The mail is built as for Gmail (M-10, M-11, M-12: plain text, or Markdown with its HTML, and the files as `multipart/mixed`), with the sender the iCloud address.
  - The message goes to `smtp.mail.me.com:587` with STARTTLS and the App-specific password of the keychain. It has a `Date` and a `Message-ID` of the app's own, and no `Bcc` header (the Bcc addresses are only in the envelope). A password that is not kept or that iCloud refuses at the login (535) answers as for Gmail's login that is needed: `send_mail` answers null, the screen reads the list again, the card of the list tells the account, and what was written stays. Any other refusal is an error in Japanese, shown on a card, and what was written stays.
  - A reply's `In-Reply-To` and `References` come from the newest mail of the thread (INBOX and Sent Messages, as the thread is read), and its subject gets "Re: " as for Gmail.
  - After the mail was taken, it is left in Sent Messages once: the folder is searched for its Message-ID, and the message is appended (read) only when iCloud has not put it there. A failure of this step is only logged, because the mail has gone; the thread then shows the reply once the copy is there, so a copy that failed to be written is not in the thread.
  - The files of a mail from an iCloud account may total what iCloud's SMTP takes (`SIZE 28319744`, 27 MB for the whole message) after base64: about 19.7 MB, which the check of a file (M-12) uses; the whole message is checked against 27 MB again before it is sent (「メールが大きすぎます（iCloud の上限は 27 MB です）」).
- The MCP tools and the Todo link (#1578).
  - `list_inbox`, `get_thread`, `archive`, `set_read` and `trash` of `tsunagiai-mcp` take an iCloud account as they take a Gmail one: `list_inbox` lists the threads of every account (an iCloud account's threads carry its address as `account`), and the other four take the iCloud address as `account` and the thread's ID, and go through the entry of `mail.rs`. When iCloud refuses the App-specific password, `list_inbox` has the account in `failed` (not in `needsLogin`, which is the Google accounts'), and the other four answer 「{address} は App 用パスワードが無効です。つなぎAIアプリの設定の iCloud タブで入れ直してください」 in place of Gmail's 「再ログインが必要です」. The description of `trash` no longer says that the thread is restored in Gmail: it says Gmail's Trash and iCloud's Deleted Messages.
  - A task made from a mail (the "Todo にする" of a row and of the thread, T-1) of an iCloud account holds `tsunagiai://mail/{address}/{thread ID}` as its description, where a Gmail account's holds Gmail's URL as before. `todoist_task_from_mail` checks the account against the accounts of every kind in `accounts.json` and refuses one that is not listed, a thread ID that is not alphanumeric and an iCloud address that holds a character other than letters, digits and `. _ + - @` (the link could not be read back as it was written). A link that is no thread of such a form is not a mail link, and a description that holds both forms is read as the Gmail one.
  - The Todo screen shows the mail icon of the row and the mail card of the detail for such a link when the address is an account the mail list knows (the Google accounts, and the iCloud accounts that have a sync position in `historyIds`), and pressing it opens the thread on the mail screen with the iCloud address and the thread's ID, as a Gmail link does (T-1).
